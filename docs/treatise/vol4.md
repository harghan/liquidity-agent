The defining geopolitical competition of the twenty-first century is the convergence of three foundational vectors:
1. **The Energy Vector**: The transition from distributed intermittent energy to massive, localized, baseload electrical power—primarily small modular nuclear reactors (SMRs) and direct natural gas turbines—dedicated entirely to high-density data centers.
2. **The Compute Vector**: The exponential scaling of compute clusters reaching gigawatt scales ($100,000$ to $1,000,000$ GPU equivalents per campus), where training runs cost tens of billions of dollars.
3. **The Epistemic Vector**: The mechanism by which civilization prices the strategic, military, and macroeconomic consequences of this immense capability.

### 11.1 The Cluster as Geopolitical Citadel

When a frontier artificial intelligence cluster consumes two gigawatts of power in an isolated desert enclave in West Texas or Saudi Arabia, that facility is not merely an enterprise data center; it is a **sovereign military asset**.

Whoever controls the frontier compute cluster controls:
- The design of novel autonomous weapons systems and electronic warfare counter-measures.
- The synthesis of synthetic molecular compounds and biochemical defenses.
- The automated discovery of zero-day cryptographic exploits against foreign infrastructure.
- The high-frequency prediction and manipulation of global financial flows.

Consequently, the physical protection of compute infrastructure will become the primary focus of national defense doctrines. The defense perimeter will not merely guard borders or oil pipelines; it will guard the switchyards, optical fiber conduits, and water cooling intakes of the compute cluster.
